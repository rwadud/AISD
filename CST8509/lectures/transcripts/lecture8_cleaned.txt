Dynamic programming, I think, is important for you. In case that term comes up, it will be important for you to know the nature of what dynamic programming is.

It is a collection of algorithms. That is maybe the first thing to get established. It is not just one thing. It is a collection of algorithms, similar to the kind of thing we have already been doing. At this point, we know what a policy is. And we can do policy evaluation, which is to compute the state value function for an arbitrary policy. We do that by iteratively following the policy and using the Bellman equation to update the values, and we keep doing this until differences from the previous iteration are small, until we get to those diminishing returns.

Now, notice that with dynamic programming, we do not have a little agent that is going out and scurrying around in the world. We have an algorithm which is sweeping the entire world all at once. And notice that it is given a perfect model. Dynamic programming, this collection of algorithms, cannot be used unless we have full knowledge of the environment, which means for every state, we already know what reward it is going to give. We do not have to try it out. We can look up that the reward for this state is such and such. One iteration of policy evaluation is to do this computation for all of the states, and then we do it again, and we do it again until it stops changing. That is policy evaluation.

Once we have the ability to see, given a policy, what the value of every state is given that policy, then we can look at making the policy better. That is called policy improvement. If we make it greedy with respect to this new value function, then that is a better policy than the one before.

So we have got policy evaluation and policy improvement, by just making it greedy with respect to the value function. And then there is this notion of policy iteration, which is to iterate policy evaluation and improvement at the same time. The drawback is that each iteration needs a full policy evaluation. So each time we want to improve the policy, we first have to go through this process of iterating and iterating until the differences are small. And that takes a while. Then we can do the policy improvement, and then we do the evaluation again. And then we do the policy improvement, and then we do the evaluation again. That is policy iteration.

The last thing we have for dynamic programming algorithms is value iteration, which is very much like policy iteration, but we just do one iteration of the evaluation. So the last two are very similar. Policy iteration does all of the evaluation until convergence, and then the improvement, and then all of the evaluation again, and then the improvement. Value iteration does one evaluation. It does not keep doing it until differences are small. It just does one. And then it makes the policy greedy with respect to the results. And then it does another evaluation, and then it makes the policy greedy with respect to those results.

So these are algorithms. For the purposes of this course, we just need to know these algorithms in the detail we have on this slide. We do not need anything more involved than that.
